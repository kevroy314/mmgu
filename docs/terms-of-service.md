# Game terms of service and where Hallkeeper draws the line

Monsters & Memories' user agreement prohibits unauthorized software that "intercepts, collects, reads, or
mines" information generated by the game, and its Play Nice Policy prohibits bots and automation. The
developers have said they may relax some of this. Until they publish a policy for third-party tools,
Hallkeeper is built around a boundary:

| Layer | What it does | Touches the game? |
|---|---|---|
| **Core hall** (always on) | Stores what people type or upload by choice: characters, items, drops, bank lists, requests, events, timers | No |
| **Add-ons** (off by default) | Read game output that a person hands over, e.g. screenshots (Screenshot Reader) or events sent by a companion tool (Ledger Sync) | Indirectly, through things a player chose to provide |
| **Companion tools** (run by each player on their own PC) | Read local game files and send them to the hall, e.g. `companion/ledger_sync.py` | Yes: they read files the game writes |

What Hallkeeper never does: read game memory, intercept network traffic, inject input, or automate play.

## How the boundary works in practice

- **Add-ons that may be affected by the game's terms carry a notice.** A leader has to read it and tick a
  box before the add-on turns on. Who accepted it and when is recorded in the audit log.
- **Companion tools ask each player.** `ledger_sync.py` prints the notice on first run and does nothing
  until the player passes `--i-understand`. It only reads files; it never modifies them.
- **Using any of this is each guild's and each player's decision.** Hallkeeper doesn't police another
  group's terms, and it doesn't pretend the question isn't there either. If the developers publish a tool
  policy, follow it; if they approve specific tools, the notices can be updated to say so.

If you maintain a fork: keep features that read game output in add-ons with an honest notice, and keep
the core free of them.
